# Code of conduct

## Our pledge

We as members, contributors and maintainers of Osprey pledge to make participation in this project a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming, diverse, inclusive and healthy community.

## Our standards

Examples of behaviour that helps:

- Being considerate about how your work affects other people, and about what a reviewer's day looks like.
- Assuming good faith. Most disagreements are a difference in context, not in competence.
- Giving and gracefully accepting constructive feedback. When a change is declined, asking why is more productive than guessing.
- Accepting responsibility, apologising when we get it wrong, and learning from it.
- Being precise. In a project about evidence, a careful description is a form of respect.
- Giving credit to the people whose work you build on.

Examples of behaviour that is not acceptable:

- Sexualised language or imagery, and sexual attention or advances of any kind.
- Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks.
- Public or private harassment.
- Publishing others' private information, such as a physical or email address, without their explicit permission.
- Dismissing or belittling a contribution because of the size of the project it came from.
- Sustained disruption of discussions.
- Any other conduct that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting.

## Scope

This code applies in all project spaces: the repository, issues, pull requests, discussions, and any other space where someone is representing the project. It also applies when an individual is officially representing the project in public.

## Enforcement

Report a problem to the maintainers by opening a confidential security advisory, which is described in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md). The maintainers will review the report, look into it, and respond in a way that is appropriate to the situation.

Maintainers who do not follow or enforce this code in good faith may face temporary or permanent repercussions as determined by the other maintainers.

## Enforcement guidelines

Community leaders follow these guidelines in determining the consequences of any action they deem in violation of this code.

### 1. Correction

**Community impact**: use of inappropriate language or other behaviour deemed unprofessional or unwelcome.

**Consequence**: a private, written warning, providing clarity about the nature of the violation and an explanation of why the behaviour was inappropriate. A public apology may be requested.

### 2. Warning

**Community impact**: a violation through a single incident or series of actions.

**Consequence**: a warning with consequences for continued behaviour. No interaction with the people involved for a specified period of time. Violating these terms may lead to a temporary or permanent ban.

### 3. Temporary ban

**Community impact**: a serious violation of community standards, including sustained inappropriate behaviour.

**Consequence**: a temporary ban from any sort of interaction or public communication with the community for a specified period of time.

### 4. Permanent ban

**Community impact**: a pattern of violation of community standards, including sustained inappropriate behaviour, harassment of an individual, or aggression toward or disparagement of classes of individuals.

**Consequence**: a permanent ban from any sort of public interaction within the community.

## Attribution

This code of conduct is written in the style of the Contributor Covenant, and its structure follows the Contributor Covenant's guidance. It is an original statement of the behaviour expected here, written for this project, and it does not reproduce any other project's text.

## Scope and privacy

The maintainers handle reports privately. A report is not published unless the person who made it asks for that, or unless the maintainers need to describe the situation in order to prevent a recurrence. Personal details from a report are not shared outside the group of maintainers who need them.
